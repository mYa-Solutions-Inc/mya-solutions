What this evaluation tests

One engine. A new substrate.

Jean was built to close the educational achievement gap. Its architecture is a deterministic mathematical reasoning stack, not a trained model. The architectural claim — validated across education, cybersecurity, clinical reasoning, sensor fusion and robotic manipulation — is that the same engine generalises across substrates without being rebuilt for each.

This SIL tests that claim in a substrate with properties the robotics work did not have: a continuously acting disturbance, a plant that must be characterised before it can be controlled, and a task where the correct answer is sometimes to do nothing.

The edge of a SIL

A SIL stops at the edge of the physical world by definition.

What it can prove is whether the decision and control logic is sound against an independent world. What it cannot prove is real-hardware performance — a separate, later category.

Architecture under test

Jean retained only the decision layer.

Everything she reasoned about was provided by independent, non-authored software.

Independent — not authored by Jean

  • Physics and hydrodynamics: Gazebo Harmonic, with the DAVE/REXROV work-class ROV model. Jean never received ground truth about forces.
  • Sensors: simulated DVL, depth, pressure and IMU, published through a ROS 2 bridge Jean did not write.
  • Actuation: the vehicle's own wrench interface.

Jean's layer

  • POWELL — fusion quality
  • CFADS — authority assessment
  • BARCA — response selection
  • RANDOLPH — intervention timing

The regulator is deterministic and stays deterministic. Jean decides whether to intervene, at what authority, and at what moment; a PID executes. This mirrors the robotics SIL, where CARVER selects the grasp and MoveIt executes it. Jean does not compute inverse kinematics there and does not compute PID arithmetic here.

Honest verdict logic

Verdicts are decoupled from whether an action was taken.

Pass

RefusedCorrectly

Impossible task, refused, reason matches.

Flagged

RefusedWrongReason

Refused, but for a reason that was not the true one.

Fail

FalseAttempt

Impossible task attempted. The marine equivalent of a false success.

Pass / Fail

HeldStation / FalseRefusal

Achievable task accepted, or wrongly declined.

A pass requires zero FalseAttempt and zero FalseRefusal. Refusing everything is not a pass; neither is attempting everything.

Independence and determinism

Physics is Gazebo's, not Jean's. Sensors are simulated by software Jean did not write. Success is measured from vehicle position observed through the simulator's own odometry, not computed by Jean. The same inputs produce the same decision — a design property of a deterministic engine.

Statistical discipline

Every result is reported as N ≥ 3 runs with spread. This is not a convention; it was forced by measurement. Three runs of an identical binary once produced a 354% spread in drift and 398% in hold time.

The integration finding

Formulas need a currency.

The most transferable result of this evaluation is not a number.

Jean's formula stack is deliberately agnostic about units — the formulas are a structure for comparison. But a comparison requires a common currency, and the integration must supply it.

Four successive architectures failed on this point before the fifth worked. Each used dimensionless weights and costs, so their ratios were arbitrary:

  • Hold cost 0.02 against CorrectSoft's 0.18 — a 10× denominator advantage chosen by nothing. Hold won from every state: 3 639 of 3 639 samples.
  • Decision margins of 0.01 logged as reasoning when they were coin flips.

The fix was to denominate everything in newtons, because that is what the actuator speaks and what the plant model is written in:

value_N = P × (force avoided later) − (force spent now)

Holding is not free — the deviation persists and grows, so the force needed later is larger. Correcting spends force now to avoid spending more later. That is a genuine economic comparison, and its margin means something.

This principle generalises to any application where the formula stack meets a physical system.

Why it worked

Every number came from the plant itself.

The controller holds depth because the forces acting on the vehicle were measured, not estimated — buoyancy by bisection, drag fitted to net force across a 65,000 N range with a 3% residual.

The proportional gain follows directly from that measured drag curve: a 1 m error commands ~3,000 N and closes at 0.1 m/s. The derivative gain is zero — the water damps the system better than any tuning could.

One constant, the effective heave mass, could not be separately identified at the achievable sample rate. We say so plainly rather than report the number the optimizer returned when it hit its bound — and the controller doesn't need it, because this plant is governed by drag.

Where it actually broke

Every defect was at an interface.

01

An upstream defect in a published research model

DAVE's REXROV model gives each thruster joint the same <pose> as its child link. Since SDF joint poses are child-relative, each revolute axis sat ~1.1 m from its thruster and the links orbited as the propellers spun. Runtime geometry disagreed with the model by up to 1.64 m and 153°.

Patched, verified to 0.000°, and disclosed in code comments rather than silently worked around.

02

The actuation interface

The vehicle's persistent-wrench topic accumulates: every message stays applied. A controller publishing each cycle therefore applied the running sum of its entire command history — roughly 8 000 wrenches over a 300 s run. The instantaneous topic applies for one physics step, so at controller rates the force acts for under 1% of the time.

The workable pattern is to publish the delta against a tracked standing value. The accumulation that broke two days of work became the mechanism that made continuous force possible.

03

Reset transients caused three separate failures

set_pose teleports position and orientation but not velocity. Every run inherited momentum from whatever preceded it. This produced: unexplained Gate 2 variance; a 354% spread across three runs of an identical binary; and, in Phase 3, run 1 having more command updates than run 2 in every trial — 586 vs 81, 1250 vs 456, 920 vs 177.

Recorded in full — the test that lied

Our own harness reported a pass it hadn't earned. We caught it by reading the numbers.

The first version of our Phase 0 test harness printed "PHASE 0 PASSED." It was wrong. The vehicle had reportedly risen under downward force, and the total response across a 1,200 N swing was a rounding error — but the monotonicity check passed on noise that happened to be ordered. Two entirely different transports produced deltas of 0.0564 and 0.0548 — the same artifact.

"A gate that passes when it should not is a more serious defect than the bug it was built to catch."

Three defects: no minimum effect size, a detection threshold below the natural variation in the vehicle's buoyant drift, and a baseline measured at a different depth than the test. None of it was caught by the test. It was caught by a person reading the numbers and asking why a vehicle rose when pushed down.

The second false result

A Phase 1 diagnostic reported drag at 11 272% of applied force, because it differentiated two adjacent samples at 10 Hz. It reported 4.00 m/s where the true average was 0.14 m/s.

Standing consequence: every gate criterion must state a minimum effect size, not only a direction or an ordering.

We record this because it is the whole discipline: an instrument that has never been caught failing has not yet been tested. The constants this program now runs on superseded the earlier assumed values by factors of 55×, 26×, and 1,500×. Every one of those corrections came from refusing to trust a green checkmark.

What this is

A software-in-the-loop demonstration that Jean's deterministic architecture — with no training and no learned policy — decides whether, how hard, and when to act on a 6-DOF underwater vehicle in an independent simulator it did not author, holding station to 8 cm and refusing the impossible with zero false attempts.

What this is not

It is not a claim of real-hardware station keeping, nor of any platform-specific operational envelope, both of which remain future work with defined paths.

The method transfers; the coefficients do not.

Known boundaries

  1. The operational envelope is REXROV's.

    The drag curves were measured on an 1 862 kg work-class vehicle. Applying them to a 17 kg inspection-class ROV would be the same error this programme has already paid for. The method transfers; the coefficients do not.

  2. Effective mass could not be measured.

    With quadratic drag at 280 412 N/(m/s)², the vehicle reaches terminal velocity in about one second; mass governs only that window, and at the achievable ~10 Hz sample rate it contains roughly ten points. The estimator was validated on synthetic data and recovered known masses exactly, but railed against its search bound on the real plant. It is recorded as not identifiable and not required — the plant is governed by drag, and drag was measured well.

  3. All refusals were caught at pre-flight.

    The in-task abort path exists and is reachable, but Phase 4's faults were detectable before commitment. Mid-run fault injection is specified and unbuilt.

  4. Position is odometry ground truth, not Jean's dead reckoning.

    This was deliberate — it isolates the control law. Moving to her own estimate, with the estimate checked against truth every run, is the next step.

The controller was never the problem. It was commanding one percent of the force the vehicle required, through a path that summed its own history, against constants wrong by factors of fifty and fifteen hundred.

Every significant defect found across this evaluation was at an interface — frames, units, clocks, sensor semantics, transport — and every one of them reported success while doing nothing. The reasoning is rarely what breaks. The interface with reality is.
